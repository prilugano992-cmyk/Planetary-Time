System Mechanics & Celestial Methods

1. Orbital Kinematics (JPL / Standish J2000)

Heliocentric 3D positions are derived using mean Keplerian orbital elements calibrated to the J2000 epoch. For any given timestamp, the system solves Kepler's equation M = E - e sin(E) to calculate true anomaly and exact orbital radial distance.

2. Rotational Orientations (IAU Coordinate Models)

Body orientation uses standard IAU planetary coordinate equations. Prime meridian locations and pole coordinates (α₀, δ₀, W) account for axial tilt, precession rates, and rotation direction (prograde vs. retrograde).

3. Subsolar Vector Calculation

The Sun's relative vector is computed by converting heliocentric coordinates to equatorial space and projecting them onto the planet's local body-fixed frame. The resulting subsolar point identifies the exact coordinates where the Sun is directly at the local zenith.

4. Local Solar Time (LST) Mapping

Local time is determined by calculating the angular difference between the selected point's longitude and the current subsolar meridian. The 360° longitudinal span is mapped to a normalized 24-hour planet-centric solar clock, where 12:00:00 aligns precisely with local solar noon.
